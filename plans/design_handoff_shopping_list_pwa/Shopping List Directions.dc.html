0Assumptions & approach
What I took from your answers
Landscape phone is the optimal shape; portrait must work equally well. Two columns always — never tabs, never a sheet. Narrow screens lose padding, not columns.
Drag is the only verb. No checkboxes except in catalog delete-mode. Right→left adds (catalog keeps the item), left→right removes.
Re-dragging an item already on the list opens a quantity prompt.
Right column: this list's own catalog, alphabetical; optionally other lists' items appended below.
Design reasoning
Row height is the whole game. A drag target needs ≥44px, but a landscape phone is ~340px tall minus a 52px bar. So rows are 44px with 15px text — dense but grabbable — and the columns scroll independently.
The gutter carries meaning. The centre divider is the add/remove threshold; it lights up during a drag so direction is unambiguous.
Two columns, one visual weight. The left list is the artifact, so it gets solid rows; the catalog is a source, so it reads lighter.
